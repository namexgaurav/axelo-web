# AXELO — Premium Editorial Technology Website

## Detailed Visual Recreation & Frontend Implementation Brief

### 1. Your Role

Act as a senior creative director, award-winning UI/UX designer, digital art director, motion designer, and expert frontend engineer specializing in premium technology brands, contemporary e-commerce, editorial websites, and immersive scrolling experiences.

Your task is to design and implement a complete, visually polished website for **AXELO**, a modern technology and desk-accessories brand.

Use the supplied website design video as the primary visual reference. Study its visual language, section compositions, typography, image arrangements, whitespace, transitions, and overall pacing. Reinterpret those characteristics for AXELO without copying the reference website's identity, content, or artwork.

The objective is to produce a website that feels like a professionally art-directed digital experience—not a generic e-commerce template.

**Priority order:**

1. Faithfully capture the reference video's visual design language.
    
2. Translate it into AXELO's premium technology brand identity.
    
3. Build a complete, functional, responsive website.
    
4. Refine the details until the implementation feels intentional, cohesive, and production-ready.

### 3. Reference Video Analysis

The reference demonstrates an editorial, image-led website with a sequence of distinct visual experiences.

Recreate the underlying design principles in the AXELO website.

#### A. Overall composition

Use a predominantly white or warm off-white canvas with generous negative space. Content should appear deliberately positioned rather than filling every available area.

The reference frequently uses:

- Compact navigation inside a wide, spacious header
    
- Large headlines positioned independently from the main visual
    
- Floating image cards that overlap one another
    
- Asymmetrical compositions with carefully controlled balance
    
- Large, isolated content blocks surrounded by whitespace
    
- Full-width or nearly full-width visual sections
    
- Alternation between sparse and visually dense sections
    

Do not make every section use the same centered container, three-column card grid, or repeated heading-and-paragraph structure.

Introduce visual variation while maintaining consistent alignment, spacing rules, and typography.

#### B. Visual rhythm

Build the page as a sequence of visual moments.

The experience should alternate between:

1. Minimal introductory composition
    
2. Large product-focused visual
    
3. Editorial brand statement
    
4. Oversized featured product or collection
    
5. Trust and credibility section
    
6. Layered product imagery
    
7. Brand philosophy and product categories
    
8. Product storytelling
    
9. Curated product discovery
    
10. Bold promotional composition
    
11. Community and brand identity
    
12. Final conversion section and footer
    

These are design directions, not rigid templates. Combine or adapt them where necessary to create the strongest AXELO experience.

Each section must feel related to the previous section, but it should not look like a duplicate of it.

### 4. Art Direction and Color System

Use a clean, neutral foundation and introduce color selectively.

**Base palette**

- Primary background: pure white, `#FFFFFF`
    
- Secondary background: soft neutral, `#F7F7F5`
    
- Primary text: near-black, `#111111`
    
- Secondary text: muted gray, `#777777`
    
- Borders and dividers: light gray, `#E8E8E8`
    

**Accent direction**

- Use AXELO's established brand accent if a logo, business card, or brand asset is supplied.
    
- Otherwise, establish a restrained electric-blue accent inspired by modern technology branding.
    
- Use strong accent colors sparingly for buttons, small labels, selected words, product details, and occasional promotional panels.
    
- Let the products and photography supply most of the additional color.
    

Do not introduce multiple unrelated accent colors merely to make the website look colorful.

The reference uses vivid colors against a light foundation. Translate this into AXELO through expressive product photography, occasional color-block sections, and distinctive product artwork.

The result should remain sophisticated, calm, and commercially credible.

- Short, confident statements

- Muted supporting text
    
- Small uppercase eyebrow labels with subtle letter spacing
    
- Clear hierarchy between headline, description, metadata, and action

## 26. Advanced Animation & Motion Recreation — Critical Requirement

### Objective

Recreate the animation style, motion behavior, visual transitions, and scrolling experience demonstrated in the supplied website reference video.

**Do not treat animation as an optional enhancement.** Motion is an integral part of the design and must be implemented alongside the layout and visual styling.

Before coding, analyze the reference video carefully. Identify each observable animation, including its trigger, direction, duration, easing, stagger order, relationship to scrolling, and final position.

Do not assume that every effect is a simple fade-in. Determine which elements move, which remain fixed, which overlap, and which change their appearance as the user scrolls.

If the original motion cannot be determined confidently from the available footage, implement the closest visually consistent interpretation instead of inventing elaborate effects.

### A. Hero Entrance Animation

Create a polished opening sequence that introduces the AXELO brand and hero composition.

- The navigation should appear cleanly, without distracting movement.
    
- The hero headline should enter with a subtle upward reveal or masked text transition.
    
- Supporting text should appear shortly after the headline.
    
- Primary and secondary CTA elements should enter in a coordinated sequence.
    
- Hero product imagery should reveal progressively, with larger visual elements receiving more emphasis.
    
- Floating product elements should settle into their intended positions without excessive bouncing or rotation.
    

Use a short, carefully staggered sequence rather than animating every element simultaneously.

Suggested starting values:

- Headline entrance: 600–900 ms
    
- Supporting text: 400–600 ms
    
- CTA entrance: 350–500 ms
    
- Hero image entrance: 700–1,000 ms
    
- Stagger interval: 80–150 ms
    

These are starting points, not mandatory values. Adjust them to match the reference's observed timing and rhythm.

### B. Scroll-Triggered Section Reveals

Implement scroll-triggered reveals for selected headings, text blocks, product images, and editorial compositions.

Possible effects:

- Fade and upward movement
    
- Image clipping or masked reveals
    
- Staggered appearance of related elements
    
- Directional movement from the left or right
    
- Subtle scale transitions
    
- Layered reveals for overlapping product compositions
    

Elements should enter at natural points during scrolling. Avoid triggering animations so early that the content appears before the section is visible, or so late that the user sees blank space.

Use `IntersectionObserver` or an equivalent efficient mechanism. Avoid expensive scroll handlers that run continuously on every frame.

Where appropriate, animate a section only once. Repeatedly replaying animations while scrolling up and down can make the website feel distracting.

### C. Editorial Image Reveals

Large product imagery should receive special attention.

Where the reference suggests an image reveal, use a clipping container and animate the image into view using a combination of clipping, opacity, or a small positional offset.

The effect should reveal the image itself, rather than merely moving an entire rectangular card onto the screen.

For large editorial images:

- Preserve the intended aspect ratio.
    
- Keep image edges crisp.
    
- Prevent the reveal container from causing layout shifts.
    
- Avoid stretching or distorting the image.
    
- Use subtle scaling only when it supports the composition.
    

For overlapping product arrangements, reveal the main image first and supporting elements afterward when this matches the visual reference.

### D. Layered and Floating Product Motion

Where the design uses overlapping products or floating imagery, establish a clear visual hierarchy.

- The main product should remain the visual anchor.
    
- Secondary objects may enter from different directions.
    
- Small positional offsets can create depth.
    
- Rotation should remain restrained.
    
- Shadow changes should be subtle and realistic.
    
- Final resting positions must be stable.
    

If the reference contains scroll-linked movement, reproduce it selectively. Do not apply parallax to every image.

Keep movement small enough that the content remains readable and the product remains the focus.

### E. Scroll-Linked Effects

Inspect the reference for effects that change continuously with the scroll position.

If present, recreate the closest equivalent for AXELO, such as:

- Images moving at slightly different rates
    
- A featured visual remaining temporarily pinned while its surrounding content changes
    
- A product composition gradually revealing additional elements
    
- Subtle image scaling during a section transition
    
- A transition between two major visual compositions
    

Only implement pinned or sticky storytelling where the reference clearly supports it and the effect improves the page.

Avoid artificial scroll-jacking. Normal browser scrolling must remain natural.

For scroll-linked motion, prefer performant transforms and opacity changes. Use a suitable animation library only when the complexity justifies the dependency.

### F. Section-to-Section Transitions

Treat transitions between major sections as part of the overall composition.

- Allow generous whitespace to establish visual pauses.
    
- Use image reveals to create continuity between adjacent sections.
    
- Transition from sparse compositions to visually rich compositions deliberately.
    
- Introduce promotional color sections with a clear visual change.
    
- Avoid excessive dividers, arbitrary zoom effects, and unnecessary animated backgrounds.
    

Not every section needs an entrance animation. Static sections are useful because they provide contrast and make the animated moments more impactful.

### G. Product Hover and Interaction Motion

Interactive elements should respond immediately and consistently.

**Product imagery**

- Apply a restrained image scale on hover where appropriate.
    
- Preserve the image's container boundaries.
    
- Avoid exaggerated zooming.
    

**Buttons**

- Use a subtle background, color, or border transition.
    
- Keep hover and pressed states consistent.
    
- Ensure keyboard focus is equally visible.
    

**Text links**

- Use a restrained underline transition or color change.
    
- Avoid unnecessary movement of surrounding content.
    

**Navigation**

- Use subtle hover feedback.
    
- Ensure menus open and close reliably.
    
- Do not make navigation dependent on hover alone.
    

Recommended transition duration for small interactions: approximately 180–300 ms.

### H. Animation Technology

Use the project's existing Svelte, TypeScript, and Tailwind setup.

Prefer:

- CSS transitions for hover states and simple reveals
    
- CSS keyframes for entrance sequences
    
- `IntersectionObserver` for viewport-triggered animations
    
- Svelte actions or reusable utilities for repeated reveal behavior
    
- A lightweight motion library when complex sequencing or scroll-linked animation genuinely requires it
    

If using GSAP or another animation library, first determine whether it is already installed. Avoid introducing multiple overlapping animation systems.

Keep animation logic reusable and maintainable. Do not duplicate complex animation code in every section.

### I. Accessibility and Performance

- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
    
- Provide a usable experience when animations are disabled.
    
- Never hide essential content indefinitely while waiting for an animation.
    
- Avoid animating layout-heavy properties when transforms or opacity can achieve the effect.
    
- Prevent layout shifts during entrance animations.
