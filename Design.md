AXELO Website Design System & UX Direction
Reference analysis: Kreo Tech
Reference pages
- Homepage: https://kreo-tech.com/
- Shop all: https://kreo-tech.com/collections/shop-all/
Purpose: Translate useful patterns from Kreo's storefront into an original, implementation-ready design direction for AXELO. This is a design brief, not a request to copy Kreo pixel-for-pixel. AXELO should retain its own identity: premium, minimal, professional, modern, accessible, and focused on desk and computer setup accessories.
1. Executive summary
Kreo's site is a product-led ecommerce storefront aimed at gaming and creator gear. Its homepage quickly routes visitors into product categories, then presents multiple product collections, deal-led merchandising, a “Shop the Look” section, social proof/content from real users, and service/support links. The Shop All page is a dense catalog with a broad product range, sorting, price filters, stock filtering, product ratings, sale prices, crossed-out MRP, discount labels, quick add-to-cart actions, and a long product list.
What AXELO should learn from the reference
- Make product discovery immediate: category entry points should be obvious.
- Use real product photography as the primary visual asset.
- Keep product cards consistent and make price, variant, rating, and purchase actions easy to scan.
- Use curated collections and bundles to help shoppers build a complete setup.
- Surface shipping, returns, warranty, order tracking, and support without making users hunt for them.
- Design the homepage and catalog as different experiences: the homepage inspires; the catalog helps users compare and decide.
What AXELO should do differently
Kreo's storefront is promotion-heavy and has many product rows and discount signals. AXELO's desired positioning is more premium and minimal. Use fewer, more intentional sections; more whitespace; calmer typography; more restrained sale treatments; and editorial product imagery. The interface should feel like a considered technology/design brand rather than a crowded gaming marketplace.
2. Reference website analysis
This analysis is based on the publicly accessible page content and structure at the URLs above. Exact pixel measurements, responsive breakpoints, animations, and visual styling should be verified in a live browser at desktop and mobile sizes before implementation.

2.1 Homepage information architecture
The homepage's visible content structure includes:
1. Promotional announcement strip with sale/freebie/return messaging.
2. Header with logo, search, account/login, and cart.
3. Hero/category gateway headed “Go Beyond Limits”.
4. Category shortcuts for keyboards, gaming mice, WFH chairs, audio/video devices, and all products.
5. Category-specific product shelves such as Gaming Keyboards and Gaming Mouse.
6. A “Shop the Look” merchandising section.
7. Audio/video and gaming-controller product groups.
8. Multi-item offer/bundle promotion.
9. A deals section.
10. Social/creator content under “Real people. Real style.”
11. Footer with quick links, support, and social channels.
Why it works
- The visitor gets clear routes into product types early.
- Repeated product shelves make a large inventory feel organized by intent.
- “Shop the Look” communicates how products work together, not just as isolated SKUs.
- The creator/social section adds community context to otherwise transactional product grids.
- Offers and support links reduce friction for price-conscious ecommerce customers.
Risks to avoid for AXELO
- Too many product shelves can make the homepage feel endless and repetitive.
- Too many simultaneous discounts can weaken a premium impression.
- A hero should communicate a strong brand idea, not only act as a gateway to a long catalog.
- Avoid showing nearly identical sections with little editorial distinction.
- Avoid making promotional messages louder than the product and brand story.
2.2 Shop All / collection page
The catalog includes:
- Search entry point.
- Filter-and-sort controls.
- Price range controls and in-stock filtering.
- Sort choices including relevance, featured, best-selling, alphabetical, price, and date.
- Product cards with product name, star rating, sale price, crossed-out MRP, discount percentage, and quick add-to-cart or choose-options action.
- A “Show more” style continuation for the long catalog.
- Footer navigation for downloads, FAQs, tracking, returns, warranty, contact, B2B, creator programs, blog, and social links.
Strengths
- Useful catalog controls are available without requiring a visitor to open each product.
- Price and discount are easy to scan.
- Product ratings help comparison.
- Quick add-to-cart shortens the purchase path for simple products.
- “Choose options” is a suitable alternative for products with variants.
Opportunities for AXELO
- Use a compact, clear filter drawer on mobile and a visible filter row or sidebar on desktop.
- Keep active filters visible and make “Clear all” easy to find.
- Show variant swatches only when they help the user make a decision.
- Use quick-add only for products that do not require a meaningful configuration step.
- Ensure the whole product card has a clear navigation target while buttons remain independently usable.
- Use pagination or a deliberate “Load more” pattern with clear loading and empty states.
- Make product count, sorting, and active filters visible and accessible.
2.3 Product card pattern
The reference card pattern communicates product identity, rating, discount, current price, former price, and a purchase action.
Recommended AXELO card anatomy
1. Product image on a consistent neutral canvas.
2. Optional small badge: NEW, BESTSELLER, or a restrained offer label.
3. Product name (maximum two lines in the grid).
4. Optional concise descriptor such as “Low-profile mechanical keyboard”.
5. Rating and review count only when real data is available.
6. Current price and former price only when a real discount exists.
7. Color/size options if relevant.
8. Primary action: Add to cart or Choose options.
Keep card heights consistent. Do not fabricate ratings, reviews, scarcity, or discounts. Use subtle borders or a very light surface change rather than heavy shadows.
2.4 Header and shopping utilities
The reference surfaces promotion messaging, account access, search, cart, and order tracking.
AXELO recommendation
- Use a slim announcement bar with one message at a time.
- Main header: AXELO wordmark, Shop, Collections, Our story, search, account, and cart.
- Keep the header calm and uncluttered; use a mobile menu with clear labels.
- Search should support product names, categories, and common synonyms.
- Cart should show item count and provide a usable empty-cart state with a clear route back to shopping.
- Account and order history should never be visually confused with checkout.
- If the header is sticky, keep its height compact and avoid covering anchor targets.
2.5 Trust, support, and community
The reference includes return/replacement messaging, order tracking, warranty, FAQs, contact information, and social content.
AXELO recommendation
- Place truthful delivery, returns, warranty, and secure-payment information near buying decisions.
- Provide dedicated Shipping, Returns, Warranty, FAQs, Contact, Track Order, and Privacy/Terms links.
- Use a compact trust row rather than a large wall of badges.
- Feature real customer desk setups, product-in-use photography, or creator reviews only with permission.
- Avoid fake testimonials, inflated review counts, or unsupported quality claims.
3. AXELO brand and art direction
Brand attributes
- Premium but accessible
- Minimal and intentional
- Modern technology
- Professional and dependable
- Calm, focused, and functional
- Global-ready, not tied to a short-lived visual trend
Design principle
Let the product and materials create the visual interest. UI chrome should be quiet, spacing generous, and product photography confident.
Brand voice
Short, direct, clear, and confident. Avoid excessive exclamation marks, gaming slang, generic “revolutionary” claims, or constant discount-led copy.
Example tone:
- “Make room for better work.”
- “Thoughtful essentials for your setup.”
- “Designed for focus. Built for everyday use.”
- “Explore the collection.”
These are directional examples, not mandatory final marketing copy.
4. Visual design system
4.1 Color palette
Use a mostly monochrome palette with one restrained accent. AXELO's exact brand color needs to be confirmed; do not assume the previously typed 006FF is a complete hex color.
Token	Suggested value	Use
color-bg	#FFFFFF	Main page background
color-surface	#F6F7F8	Product image canvas, quiet panels
color-surface-raised	#FFFFFF	Menus, drawers, cards
color-text	#111315	Primary text
color-text-muted	#686D73	Descriptions, metadata
color-border	#E5E7EB	Dividers, subtle outlines
color-accent	Confirm AXELO brand accent	Links, focus, selected states
color-success	Accessible dark green	Confirmed success states only
color-error	Accessible dark red	Validation and error states only


Rules:
- Use black/near-black and white as the foundation.
- Avoid gradients, neon glows, and decorative color splashes by default.
- Reserve accent color for meaningful actions and active states.
- Maintain WCAG-conscious contrast; do not rely on color alone to communicate status.
- Do not use red sale badges everywhere. Promotions should be selective and truthful.
4.2 Typography
Use a clean contemporary sans-serif. Prefer a high-quality variable font already available in the project or a dependable web font such as Inter or Geist.
Suggested hierarchy:
- Display/hero: 52–72 px desktop; 36–44 px mobile; tight but readable line-height.
- H1: 44–56 px desktop; 34–40 px mobile.
- H2: 32–40 px desktop; 28–32 px mobile.
- H3: 22–28 px.
- Body: 16–18 px with comfortable line-height.
- UI labels: 13–15 px.
- Product title: 15–17 px.
- Price: 16–18 px, semibold.
- Eyebrow/overline: 11–13 px, medium weight, modest letter spacing.
Use sentence case for headings and buttons. Avoid making every heading uppercase. Keep line lengths controlled and ensure mobile text never feels cramped.
4.3 Spacing and layout
Use a consistent 4 px base scale, with larger editorial gaps:
- 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 80, 96, 120 px.
Layout guidance:
- Desktop content max-width: approximately 1280–1440 px.
- Desktop page gutters: 40–64 px depending on viewport.
- Tablet gutters: 24–32 px.
- Mobile gutters: 16–20 px.
- Section spacing: 72–112 px desktop; 48–72 px mobile.
- Card gaps: 16–24 px.
- Use whitespace to separate ideas, not just boxes and borders.
4.4 Corners, borders, and shadows
- Use 8–14 px radii for functional cards and controls.
- Large feature media may use 12–20 px radius if consistent with the art direction.
- Prefer thin neutral borders to heavy shadows.
- Use shadows sparingly for floating menus, drawers, and overlays only.
- Avoid the “every element is a rounded pill” look; reserve pill shapes for tags and selected compact controls.
4.5 Iconography
Use one consistent outline icon family. Keep icons simple, stroke weight consistent, and labels visible where the meaning may be unclear. Do not use emoji as interface icons. Icon-only buttons require accessible names and visible focus states.
5. Page-by-page experience
5.1 Homepage
Recommended section order:
1. Announcement bar
   - One short, factual message: shipping threshold, launch offer, or support promise.
   - Optional dismiss control if the message is not essential.
   - Avoid multiple rotating messages that move too quickly.
2. Header
   - AXELO wordmark.
   - Shop / Collections / Our Story.
   - Search, Account, Cart.
   - Responsive mobile navigation.
3. Hero
   - Strong editorial product photograph or composed desk scene.
   - Short headline that states the brand benefit.
   - One primary CTA (Shop the collection) and optional secondary CTA (Discover AXELO).
   - Keep text contrast high and avoid placing critical copy over busy imagery.
   - On mobile, use a purpose-cropped image rather than shrinking the desktop composition.
4. Category shortcuts
   - Desk mats, keyboards, mice, cable management, and setup essentials as inventory supports them.
   - Use consistent image crops and short labels.
   - Do not display categories that have no available products.
5. Featured / launch collection
   - 3–4 products on desktop, 2 on mobile where practical.
   - Prioritize the current launch product rather than a large assortment of unrelated items.
   - Include a clear View all link.
6. Brand/product story
   - A short statement about function, material, comfort, focus, or thoughtful design.
   - Pair with close-up product photography or a clean lifestyle image.
   - Keep claims specific and verifiable.
7. Shop the setup / curated bundle
   - Show products used together in a coherent desk scene.
   - Link each product in the setup and offer a bundle only if pricing and inventory support it.
   - This should feel editorial, not like a forced upsell.
8. Product benefits
   - Three concise points maximum, based on actual product attributes.
   - Use simple diagrams or macro photography when they explain construction or function.
9. Social proof
   - Real customer photos, verified reviews, or creator content.
   - If AXELO is pre-launch and has no reviews yet, omit this section or use an honest behind-the-scenes section instead.
10. Newsletter / launch updates
    - Clear value exchange, simple email field, privacy reassurance.
    - No aggressive pop-up on first page load.
11. Footer
    - Shop, About, Help, Policies, Contact, and social links.
    - Include business contact information and relevant policy links.
    - Keep the footer structured and easy to scan.
5.2 Shop All / collection page
Desktop:
- Page title and one-line collection description.
- Product count and sort control.
- Filters in a left sidebar or a compact filter toolbar.
- Product grid of 3–4 columns depending on viewport.
- Sticky filter sidebar only if it improves long-list navigation and does not dominate the page.
Mobile:
- Page title, result count, and two clear controls: Filter and Sort.
- Filter drawer with grouped options and sticky Show results / Clear all actions.
- Two-column product grid where image and product name remain legible.
- Avoid tiny tap targets and crowded filter chips.
Useful filters for AXELO as inventory grows:
- Category
- Price
- Availability
- Color
- Size/dimensions
- Compatibility or connection type where relevant
Sorting:
- Featured
- Newest
- Best selling (only when supported by real sales data)
- Price: low to high
- Price: high to low
Collection page states:
- Loading/skeleton state
- No products in collection
- No results for selected filters
- Filtered results
- Pagination or load-more state
- Recoverable network/API error
5.3 Product detail page
Recommended structure:
1. Breadcrumbs.
2. Product image gallery with zoom and thumbnails.
3. Product title, short benefit statement, real rating/review count if available.
4. Price and genuine discount information.
5. Variant selection with clear selected state.
6. Availability and delivery estimate when reliable.
7. Primary Add to cart action.
8. Short returns/warranty/shipping reassurance.
9. Key features with scannable labels.
10. Detailed description, dimensions, materials, care, compatibility, and package contents.
11. Product-in-use images and close-ups.
12. Related products or a relevant setup bundle.
13. Reviews and FAQs when available.
Mobile recommendation:
- Keep purchase controls easy to reach; a sticky bottom add-to-cart bar may be used if it does not obscure content or accessibility controls.
- Show validation messages next to the relevant option.
- Do not add an item to cart until required variants are selected.
5.4 Cart and checkout
- Cart drawer can support quick review, but a full cart page should remain available.
- Clearly show item, variant, quantity, price, removal, subtotal, and shipping/tax messaging.
- Do not surprise users with hidden fees.
- Keep checkout focused and minimize unnecessary steps.
- Guest checkout should remain possible unless a genuine business requirement prevents it.
- If AXELO requires login for saved addresses or order history, explain why and preserve cart contents through authentication.
- Show clear success, pending, and failure states for payment.
5.5 Account and authentication
If email OTP and phone OTP are implemented:
- Explain the selected sign-in method.
- Normalize phone numbers with country code and validate email format.
- Show OTP expiry/resend countdown and clear error messages.
- Rate-limit OTP requests and avoid revealing whether an account exists.
- Provide loading, retry, expired-code, invalid-code, and success states.
- Never expose Supabase service-role credentials in client code.
- Ask only for profile information needed for the experience.
- Address forms should have explicit labels and preserve user input after recoverable errors.
6. Interaction and motion
Motion should feel quiet, responsive, and purposeful. It should reinforce hierarchy and state, not call attention to itself.
Recommended micro-interactions
- Buttons: subtle color/background transition over 150–220 ms.
- Product cards: small image scale or image swap on hover, approximately 1.01–1.03 scale; disable hover-only behavior on touch devices.
- Navigation: short fade/slide for menus and drawers.
- Add to cart: immediate button feedback, cart count update, and accessible confirmation.
- Filter changes: clear selected-state feedback; do not unexpectedly jump the page.
- Gallery: smooth image transition without delaying interaction.
- Accordions: quick height/opacity transition with reduced-motion support.
- Loading: stable skeletons that avoid layout shifts.
Motion rules
- Typical duration: 150–250 ms for UI; up to 400 ms for larger editorial transitions.
- Use restrained easing; avoid springy/bouncy movement.
- Do not autoplay sound or force long intro animations.
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
- Every interactive control must work with keyboard, touch, and pointer input.
- Motion must never be the only signal that a state changed.
Avoid
- Large parallax effects that make shopping slower.
- Scroll hijacking.
- Cursor-following effects that reduce usability.
- Excessive reveal animations on every section.
- Animations that delay access to product information or checkout.
7. Responsive behavior
Design mobile-first and verify at these representative widths:
- Small mobile: 320–374 px
- Standard mobile: 375–430 px
- Tablet: 768–1023 px
- Laptop: 1024–1439 px
- Large desktop: 1440 px and above
Rules:
- No horizontal overflow.
- Product images maintain consistent aspect ratio and avoid accidental cropping of the product.
- Navigation collapses to a labeled menu on smaller screens.
- Filter and sort controls become mobile-friendly drawers/sheets.
- Buttons and important touch targets should generally be around 44 × 44 px or larger.
- Text, price, and controls must not collide at narrow widths.
- Use responsive image sources and appropriately sized assets.
- Verify portrait and landscape mobile layouts.
8. Accessibility and quality bar
Target WCAG 2.2 AA where practical.
- Semantic headings in a logical order.
- Keyboard-accessible menus, dialogs, filters, galleries, and cart.
- Visible focus indicator with adequate contrast.
- Correct labels and accessible names for form fields and icon buttons.
- Useful alt text for meaningful product imagery; decorative images should have empty alt text.
- Error messages associated with the relevant form controls.
- Status announcements for cart updates and form results.
- Color contrast checked for text, controls, badges, and focus states.
- Do not communicate sale status, selection, or errors by color alone.
- Respect reduced motion and browser zoom.
- Test the site using keyboard-only navigation and a screen reader.
9. Content and product photography
Photography direction
- Realistic, high-quality product images with accurate color and proportions.
- Neutral backgrounds: white, warm grey, graphite, or natural desk materials.
- Soft directional light, believable shadows, and clean composition.
- Mix isolated product images, macro detail shots, and in-context desk scenes.
- Maintain consistent scale and camera angle across product cards.
- For desk mats and accessories, show material texture, edge finishing, dimensions, and actual use.
- Avoid overly busy RGB/gaming scenes unless the product specifically requires that context.
Image rules
- Use a consistent product-card ratio (for example, 4:3 or 1:1) and document it in the component system.
- Use larger, wider images for editorial hero sections.
- Optimize file sizes and serve modern formats where supported.
- Reserve image space before loading to prevent layout shifts.
- Never stretch images to fill a box.
- Do not use reference-brand imagery or logos as AXELO assets without permission.

11. Suggested technical implementation guidance
The design brief is technology-agnostic. For an AXELO storefront using Svelte + TypeScript + Tailwind CSS:
- Use Svelte components for reusable UI and TypeScript types for product, collection, variant, cart, and customer data.
- Define design tokens as CSS variables and map them to Tailwind utilities.
- Keep product cards, price display, filters, and cart logic reusable across homepage and collection pages.
- Use semantic HTML and progressive enhancement.
- Keep secrets and privileged API keys on the server; never expose Supabase service-role keys in browser bundles.
- Use Supabase Row Level Security for user-scoped data and validate important operations server-side.
- Keep inventory, price, discounts, and order totals authoritative on the server/database.
- Optimize product images, lazy-load below-the-fold media, and avoid layout shifts.
- Add analytics only with appropriate privacy practices and consent requirements.
- Test empty, loading, error, unauthenticated, and authenticated states—not only the ideal flow.
Do not introduce a new framework or state-management library solely to implement this visual direction. Prefer the existing project conventions unless there is a concrete reason to change them.
12. Performance targets
Use Core Web Vitals as practical quality goals, not as guaranteed outcomes:
- LCP: aim for 2.5 seconds or less under representative conditions.
- INP: aim for 200 ms or less.
- CLS: aim for 0.1 or less.
Implementation priorities:
- Compress and correctly size hero/product images.
- Avoid shipping large animation libraries for simple transitions.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold images and nonessential embeds.
- Keep the first viewport useful before third-party widgets load.
- Avoid layout shifts caused by fonts, image dimensions, banners, and cart overlays.
- Keep filters and cart interactions responsive on lower-powered mobile devices.
13. Ecommerce trust and conversion
- Show clear pricing and explain genuine discounts.
- State shipping thresholds, estimated delivery, returns, and warranty terms accurately.
- Provide visible contact and order-tracking paths.
- Use real reviews and product specifications.
- Keep checkout friction low and provide clear payment status feedback.
- Do not use fake countdowns, false stock scarcity, fabricated reviews, or misleading crossed-out prices.
- Make empty states helpful rather than dead ends.
- Keep cart contents available if a shopper signs in during the purchase journey.

16. Final direction
Build AXELO as a quietly premium desk-setup brand. Borrow the reference's clear category navigation, organized merchandising, scannable product cards, curated setup concept, and visible customer support. Replace the reference's high-density, discount-forward merchandising with confident whitespace, precise product photography, a restrained monochrome system, a small number of purposeful sections, and transparent product information.
The result should feel polished and useful before it feels animated. Every visual detail should help a shopper understand the product, trust the brand, or make a decision.
